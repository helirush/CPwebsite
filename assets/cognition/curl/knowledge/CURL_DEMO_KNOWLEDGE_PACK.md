## What the journey is trying to show
1. Cognition Partnership begins with the individual human.
2. A persistent Partner can reason with the human over time as the human explores, questions, creates, changes direction, rejects ideas, and discovers new ones.
3. The Partner helps preserve continuity across that journey.
4. Over time a Living Library can emerge — not merely documents, but accumulated understanding that can be recovered and continued.
5. Knowledge may extend beyond one lifetime; descendants may someday explore appropriate portions of experiences, reasoning, discoveries, and stories.
6. A human may create something (craft, consulting, research, engineering, teaching, entrepreneurship, business). Some knowledge can be intentionally separated from the private human library into a purpose-specific library.
7. A Cognition Host can then represent an authorized body of knowledge to other humans.
8. The Host does not need everything the individual ever thought. Knowledge branches with different purposes and permissions.
9. Examples of branching possibility:
   - small business Host around decades of practical experience
   - engineering org preserving design/revision/decision reasoning
   - family generational knowledge
   - researcher specialized knowledge environment
   - organization Host helping visitors interrogate authorized knowledge conversationally

## Cognitive alignment
Successful Cognition Partnership requires more than model intelligence.
Human and Partner must maintain sufficient shared state.
Two participants can hold similar information yet become cognitively out of phase if operating from different versions of a problem, artifact, or conclusion.
Especially important with images, drawings, code, designs, and other artifacts.
A human may mentally reject an earlier design and move on; if the Partner later encounters the rejected artifact without knowing it was superseded, it can resurrect an obsolete state.
That is **cognitive phase error**.
Good partnership preserves meaningful state transitions, not only conclusions:
- What was considered?
- What was learned?
- What was rejected?
- Why was it rejected?
- What superseded it?
- What is now authoritative?
This is one reason Living Libraries matter.
